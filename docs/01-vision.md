# 01 — Vision

## The problem

Habit and life-gamification apps (Habitica, LifeRPG, Do It Now) retain users for weeks, not years. The XP loop works at first — then collapses, because XP buys nothing. Games solved this decades ago: progression systems only sustain engagement when they pay out something the player actually wants (skins, currency, gear). Habit apps copied the progression and dropped the payout.

Meanwhile, commitment-device apps (Beeminder, stickK) prove that real money changes behavior — but they are punishment-framed: fail and lose your money. That works for a small, masochistic niche and repels everyone else.

## The insight

Combine the two, but flip the frame:

> **Your own money, locked in a vault, unlocked by your own effort, spent on things you already wanted.**

- The reward is real (an iFood order, an Amazon item from your wishlist) — not a sticker.
- The reward is self-funded — the app never pays out its own money, so unit economics don't bleed.
- The frame is positive — success unlocks a treat; failure just means waiting longer. Nobody is punished.
- Cheating is self-defeating — a user who fakes a quest only unlocks their own money faster. There is no payout fraud surface.

## Who it's for

**Primary persona: the gamer who wants their life to feel like their games.** 18–35, grew up on RPGs, already speaks in quests/XP/grinding, has disposable income and a wishlist. They don't want a productivity app — they want a game where the player character is them.

This is why the entire visual identity is **pixel art** (see [08-design-direction.md](08-design-direction.md)): the app should feel like opening Stardew Valley, not opening a to-do list.

Secondary personas: fitness grinders (quests = workouts, verified by Strava/Health), students (study streaks), savers (the vault doubles as friction-savings — money locked away from impulse spending earns yield-free discipline).

## Positioning

| | Habitica / LifeRPG | Beeminder / stickK | **QuestVault** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reward | Virtual only | Avoiding loss | Real items, self-funded |
| Money frame | None | Punishment | Celebration |
| Aesthetic | Pixel-lite / utilitarian | Spreadsheet | Full pixel-art game |
| App's payout risk | None | None | **None** (user's own funds) |

**Tagline candidates:**
- "Earn your loot."
- "Your life. Your gold. Your quests."
- "The game where the loot is real."

## What success looks like

- Year 1: the gamification loop alone retains (D30 ≥ 25%) and converts to paid (≥ 5% of MAU).
- Year 2: ≥ 30% of subscribers fund a vault; average vault ≥ R$150; redemption moments shared organically on social media ("I ran 40 km this month and the app bought me my game" is inherently viral).
- Year 3: the app card is how members treat themselves — interchange + float make every funded user profitable regardless of subscription.
